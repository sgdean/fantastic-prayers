Project update from Steven Dean · September 2026

The work is running again.

Fantastic Prayers was built with CD-ROM technology that today's computers no longer support. I can experience the original again because it is running in an emulator on my laptop.

A working proposal for discussion with the artists

In brief

The original CD-ROM and its files are preserved, but the technology it depends on is no longer part of today's computers. It now runs in an on my laptop, which means I can experience it again and use it as a reference. I have taken the CD-ROM apart without changing any of those original files. And I have built a browser prototype of one environment, Walls That Speak. We have enough now to make real choices about what comes next.

A note from Steven

I started this work because I wanted to know whether Fantastic Prayers could still be experienced, not just remembered. Getting it to run again changed the project. I could see and hear the work again and navigate through environments I had not experienced in years.

Once it was running, I had another question. Could we understand it well enough to bring it back to life using modern web browser technology, so people could experience it again? To answer that question, I started taking the CD-ROM apart to learn what was inside.

What follows isn't a finished proposal. I'm still in the middle of the work. It is an invitation to tell me where my memory is wrong, and the beginning of a conversation about fidelity, transformation, and what a future life for this project might look like.

Steven Dean

Work to date

What we have now.

01

The original runs again

The original installer works. The CD-ROM opens and plays in an emulator on my laptop. That gives us a reference for what the released work did, which isn't always the same as what we remember or what the intended.

02

The CD-ROM has been disassembled

I went through all 66 files without changing the originals. The images, sounds, videos, scripts, interactions, , logic, relationships, and unresolved questions are now cataloged by environment. I also created a for each file, so we can confirm none have changed.

03

Walls has a browser prototype

I built a prototype of Walls That Speak. It showed me that the imagery, audio, video, hotspots, cursor behavior, and memory of an environment can be reassembled with modern web browser technology.

Working browser prototype

Enter the walls.

The Walls prototype is not complete. I still have the Kitchen subsection to build. But it helped me understand how much work one environment takes, gave me a useful set of lessons, and showed me what it could mean to experience this work in a browser.

What comes next

Three next steps.

Interactive excerpts

Before taking on the whole work, we can rebuild one representative screen or moment from each environment for this site. An excerpt might include a rollover, hotspot, animation, sound, or video. Together, these excerpts would let us test different interactions and give viewers a direct sense of how each environment behaves.

A proposal for funding

I will keep building out this site as a proposal we can share with potential funders. It will bring together the history, the work completed so far, the excerpts, the scope, the costs, the timing, and the choices involved.

With funding in place, we could rebuild the released experience using modern HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. We would use the emulator, original code, design document, recovered assets, and our own memories without pretending they are all the same kind of evidence.

What I'd like to discuss next.

Constance, Tony, and Stephen: I am building this site first as a place for us to think together.

I'll keep fleshing out this proposal so we have something concrete to share with potential funders. As it takes shape, I want to make sure it reflects what matters most to each of you and gives us a shared understanding of what we are proposing, what it will cost, how long it could take, and the choices involved.

Next I plan to add short recordings from every principal environment, readable sections from the original design document, fuller histories and contributor pages, and small interactive excerpts we can look at together.
